# A Temperate 2.5 Earth-radius Planet Candidate on an 8.4-day Orbit around the M Dwarf TIC 71150324

Advait Paliwal
Independent researcher, Sterling Heights, MI 48314, USA
advaitpaliwal@gmail.com

## Abstract

I report a transit-like signal on TIC 71150324 (2MASS J04293004−1654557), an M dwarf at 95.0 pc, in TESS full-frame-image photometry from six sectors spanning 2018–2026. A transit fit gives a period of 8.422169 ± 0.000017 d, a depth of 2277 ± 300 ppm and a duration of 1.84 h, implying a radius of 2.49 ± 0.19 R⊕ and an equilibrium temperature of 480 ± 21 K. The star is not a TOI or CTOI, and no SPOC threshold-crossing event is listed. Seventeen transits recovered in four independent extractions give consistent odd and even depths and no secondary eclipse. Difference images exclude all seven Gaia sources within 60″. Same-CCD comparison stars show no common dip. With pixel exclusions and Gaia companion limits, TRICERATOPS gives FPP = 0.033 ± 0.003. The candidate is not validated and awaits high-resolution imaging.

## Star and search

TIC 71150324 (Stassun et al. 2019) has T = 12.80, Teff = 3586 ± 157 K, R★ = 0.493 ± 0.015 R☉, M★ = 0.491 ± 0.020 M☉ and Gaia DR3 RUWE 1.05 (Gaia Collaboration et al. 2023). ExoFOP lists no imaging or spectroscopic follow-up for the star, and it has no TESS 2-minute data. I searched TESS-SPOC (Caldwell et al. 2020) and QLP (Huang et al. 2020a, 2020b; Kunimoto et al. 2021) full-frame light curves of nearby cool dwarfs lacking 2-minute data with Transit Least Squares (Hippke & Heller 2019) over 1–12 d. Each detection had to pass three gates before any false-positive analysis: the transit had to appear in both raw and corrected flux, the pipelines had to agree on its depth, and difference images had to place it on the target.

## Transit fit

I fitted native-cadence photometry from Sectors 5 (1800 s, TESS-SPOC), 32 (600 s, TESS-SPOC), 98 (200 s, QLP) and 106–108 (200 s, aperture photometry on TESScut images; Brasseur et al. 2019). The fit used batman (Kreidberg 2015) and emcee (Foreman-Mackey et al. 2013), with exposure integration, a circular orbit, a TIC stellar-density prior and Claret (2017) quadratic limb darkening. It gives P = 8.4221690 (+0.0000171/−0.0000158) d, T0 = 2461256.4492 ± 0.0027 BJD_TDB, Rp/R★ = 0.0464 ± 0.0033, a/R★ = 27.9 ± 0.5, b = 0.68 (+0.05/−0.06), depth 2277 (+314/−281) ppm and T14 = 1.84 ± 0.11 h (Figure 1). This implies Rp = 2.49 ± 0.19 R⊕, a = 0.064 AU, insolation 8.9 ± 2.1 S⊕ and Teq = 480 ± 21 K (zero albedo, full redistribution).

## Vetting

Seventeen complete transits give a pooled depth of 2148 ± 158 ppm. After each group's errors are inflated by √χ²ᵣ, the signal remains 8.4σ, with odd and even depths agreeing to 0.07σ and a phase-0.5 depth of −69 ± 388 ppm. No single transit carries more than 12% of the weight, and removing any one transit leaves at least 11.5σ (before inflation). Combined depths are 2525 ± 261 ppm (TESS-SPOC S5/S32), 1891 ± 235 ppm (QLP), 2320 ± 321 ppm (TARS) and 1938 ± 234 ppm (TESScut S106–108). Raw and corrected flux agree in every pipeline sector. A post-egress residual seen in the stacked fit comes entirely from the TESScut sectors; the pipeline sectors give −25 ± 489 ppm in the same window. Twenty brightness-matched comparison stars per sector on the same camera and CCD show no common dip in S106–108. The fraction with |S/N| > 3 at the target ephemeris (0–5%) matches shifted-ephemeris nulls (2–3%), and in S5, S32 and S98 only 3 of 64, 3 of 59 and 5 of 66 comparison stars exceed |S/N| = 3. No accepted transit falls within 0.25 d of a SPOC momentum dump.

Difference images fitted with the TESS PRF place the in-transit flux deficit on the target in Sectors 5, 32 and 98. Each of the seven Gaia sources within 60″ is disfavoured relative to the target by a summed Δχ² of at least 45.9, and none is preferred.

## False-positive probability

TRICERATOPS (Giacalone & Dressing 2020; Giacalone et al. 2021) was run ten times with 10⁶ draws each. The seven excluded neighbours were removed, and the same runs applied companion limits from Gaia astrometry and imaging via MOLUSC (Wood et al. 2021). This gives FPP = 0.0329 ± 0.0034 and NFPP = 4 × 10⁻⁶. Most of the FPP (0.027) comes from a planet orbiting an unresolved bound companion. Eclipsing-binary scenarios at twice the period sum to 0.005. The star has no high-resolution imaging, and Collier et al. (2026) find that small validated planets in their imaging sample needed contrast curves, so I make no validation claim.

## Summary

TIC 71150324.01 is a 2.5 R⊕ candidate with Teq ≈ 480 K, on an 8.4 d orbit around an early M dwarf. Its 0.23% transit makes it accessible to ground-based photometry. High-resolution imaging and radial velocities are the next steps. Code and data are at https://github.com/advaitpaliwal/TIC71150324.
